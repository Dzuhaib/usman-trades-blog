# Blog Writing Requirements — usmantrades.co.uk

> Give this file to any AI model (ChatGPT, Claude, Gemini, etc.) before asking it to write a blog post for usmantrades.co.uk. The AI must read and follow every section below before producing a single word.

---

## 1. Word Count

- Every blog post must be **1,900+ words minimum** (excluding headings, captions, and disclaimers).
- Do not pad with filler or repeat the same point in different words just to hit the count — add real value: examples, data, context, historical comparisons, trader takeaways.
- Break the post into clear sections (see Structure below) so length feels earned, not stretched.

---

## 2. Banned "AI-Sounding" Words & Phrases

The AI must **actively scan its own draft** and remove/replace every instance of these before finalizing. These words are dead giveaways of AI writing and must never appear.

**Banned words:**
delve, boast/boasts, tapestry, testament, landscape (as in "the trading landscape"), navigate (as in "navigate the markets"), realm, elevate, unlock, unleash, robust, seamless, seamlessly, dynamic, vibrant, intricate, myriad, plethora, paramount, pivotal, crucial (overused), underscore/underscores, foster, harness, leverage (as a verb, overused), synergy, holistic, game-changer, groundbreaking, cutting-edge, transformative, ever-evolving, ever-changing, in the ever-evolving world of, fast-paced world, digital age, in today's world, in this day and age, whether you're a beginner or an expert, it's important to note that, it's worth noting that, needless to say, without a doubt, in conclusion, in summary, to sum up, overall, at the end of the day, when it comes to, dive into/dive deep, unpack, embark, journey (as a metaphor for learning), empower, empowering, aligns with, resonates with, showcases, comprehensive, multifaceted, nuanced, complexities, endeavor, meticulous/meticulously, top-notch, first and foremost, last but not least, in a nutshell, the world of [X], a testament to, stands as, serves as a reminder, plays a crucial role, cannot be overstated, is key to, unveil, revolutionize, redefine, next-level, one-stop-shop, game plan (overused), rest assured, look no further, buckle up, fasten your seatbelt.

**Banned sentence patterns:**
- "Whether you're a beginner or a seasoned trader, this guide..."
- "In today's fast-paced financial world..."
- "Let's dive into..."
- "It's important to note that..."
- Starting a conclusion with "In conclusion" or "To sum up"
- Overuse of rhetorical questions as section openers ("But what does this mean for you?")
- Three-item lists that always follow the same rhythm ("not only X, but also Y, and ultimately Z")
- Excessive em-dashes used as a crutch for every other sentence

**Rule:** If a sentence could appear in literally any blog post about any topic with the nouns swapped out, rewrite it. It must sound like it was written by someone who actually trades and follows these specific markets.

## 3. Human-Written Style & Tone

Write like a real financial writer/trader typing at their desk, not a content-generation engine. Follow these rules:

- **Vary sentence length.** Mix short, punchy sentences with longer, detailed ones. Real writing doesn't have uniform rhythm.
- **Use contractions** naturally (it's, don't, you'll, here's) — don't write overly formal or stiff.
- **Have an opinion.** A real analyst says "this looks overextended" or "I'd be cautious chasing this move" — not just neutral fence-sitting on every point.
- **Use specific numbers, dates, and levels** instead of vague statements. Say "gold rejected $2,650 twice this week" instead of "gold faced resistance recently."
- **Avoid perfect symmetry.** Don't force every section into the exact same word count or bullet structure — real articles breathe unevenly.
- **Start sections with a real observation, not a template.** Avoid always opening with a definition or a rhetorical question.
- **Write like you're explaining it to a trader friend**, not lecturing a classroom. Confident, direct, slightly conversational — never robotic or overly polished.
- **Light imperfection is fine.** One-line paragraphs for emphasis, the occasional fragment for punch ("Not this week.") — humans write like this; AI usually doesn't.
- **No excessive hedging.** Avoid stacking qualifiers like "it could potentially perhaps suggest" — real writers commit to a read while still noting risk.

---

## 4. Financial Writer Expert Tone

- Write as an experienced markets/trading analyst who understands technical analysis, macro drivers, and retail trader psychology — not a generalist copywriter.
- Reference **real, current context**: interest rate expectations, USD strength/weakness, risk sentiment, key support/resistance, upcoming data releases (NFP, CPI, FOMC), seasonal patterns — whatever is relevant to the asset.
- Use correct trading terminology naturally: liquidity, order blocks, supply/demand zones, RSI divergence, volume, breakout/breakdown, consolidation, ATH, DXY, correlation, risk-on/risk-off — but explain briefly for beginners without dumbing it down for experienced traders.
- Include a **balanced risk perspective** — never pure hype ("this will definitely moon") and never pure fear. Present the bullish case, the bearish case, and the key level that would invalidate the current view.
- End relevant posts with a **short, standard risk disclaimer** (trading involves risk, not financial advice) — keep it brief, one or two lines, not a legal wall of text.
- Cite believable, relevant data points (price levels, % moves, historical comparisons) rather than vague claims like "many analysts believe."

---

## 5. Structure (use as a flexible template, not a rigid formula)

1. **Hook / Intro** (100–150 words) — a real, specific opening observation about the current market situation. No generic definitions.
2. **Context / Background** (200–300 words) — why this topic matters right now.
3. **Main Analysis** (800–1,000 words) — technical breakdown, key levels, fundamental drivers, split into 2–4 subheadings.
4. **What Traders Should Watch** (250–350 words) — upcoming catalysts, key levels to watch, scenarios.
5. **Risk Factors / Bear Case vs Bull Case** (200–300 words) — balanced view.
6. **Takeaway / Closing thought** (100–150 words) — a direct, confident closing observation. Not "In conclusion."
7. **Disclaimer** (1–2 lines).

Use H2/H3 headings that are specific to the content (e.g., "Why $2,680 Is the Level That Matters" instead of generic "Analysis").

## 6. Images — Sourcing & Duplication Rules

Before generating or requesting any image, the AI must:

1. **Check existing images first.** Review previously used images/image library for this blog (folder, CMS media library, or prior post history) to avoid reusing the same image twice across posts.
2. **Match images to the specific asset/topic**, not generic stock photos of "business people shaking hands" or "stock market graphs" with no relevance:
   - Bitcoin / crypto post → search and pull **Bitcoin price chart**, **BTC/USD chart**, or crypto-market-specific images.
   - Gold post → search and pull **XAUUSD chart**, **gold price chart**, or gold bar/market images.
   - Forex pair post (e.g., EUR/USD) → search that **specific pair's chart** or relevant currency imagery.
   - Stock/index post → pull relevant **index chart** or **company-specific** imagery.
3. **Use Pixabay (or the designated stock source)** to search topic-specific queries — e.g., "bitcoin chart," "XAUUSD gold chart," "forex trading chart" — rather than one generic query for every post.
4. **No duplicate images across posts.** Track which images have been used previously; if a match/near-duplicate is found, search again with a refined query or pick an alternate result.
5. Prefer images that show **actual price charts, candlesticks, or asset-relevant visuals** over generic "finance" stock photography, since this reinforces expert/analytical tone.
6. Include **3–5 relevant images** per post, spaced naturally between sections (not all bunched at the top).
7. Always add descriptive **alt text** for each image mentioning the asset (e.g., "XAUUSD gold price chart showing resistance at $2,650") for SEO.
