# What is Forex Trading? A Simple Guide to the Global Market

The Foreign Exchange market, or Forex, is the largest financial market in the world, with over $7 trillion traded every single day. To put that in perspective, the New York Stock Exchange handles a mere fraction of that volume. But what actually happens in the Forex market? Is it just people swapping currency at the airport? Not quite.

In this guide, we will demystify Forex trading and explain how you can participate in this global arena with a professional mindset.

## The Core Concept: Trading Value, Not Paper

When you trade Forex, you are essentially betting on the economic health of one country relative to another. You are always trading a Currency Pair. For example, in the EUR/USD pair, you are buying the Euro and selling the US Dollar simultaneously. If you believe the European economy will outperform the US economy, you Go Long (Buy) EUR/USD. If you think the US Dollar will strengthen, you Go Short (Sell) EUR/USD.

Real World Example: In 2022, the European Central Bank raised interest rates for the first time in over a decade, signaling a shift toward tighter monetary policy. Traders who understood that this would strengthen the Euro relative to the Dollar went long on EUR/USD. The pair rallied from 1.03 to 1.10 over the following months, rewarding those who understood the fundamental dynamic.

*   **Base Currency (EUR):** The first currency in the pair. This is the currency you are buying or selling.
*   **Quote Currency (USD):** The second currency in the pair. This tells you how much of the quote currency you need to buy one unit of the base currency.

If the EUR/USD rate is 1.1000, it means you need $1.10 to buy 1 Euro. If you think the Euro will strengthen to 1.1200, you buy EUR/USD at 1.1000 and profit from the difference when you sell at 1.1200.

## Who Moves the Market?

Unlike the stock market, which is centralized on an exchange, Forex is a decentralized or Over The Counter (OTC) market. The participants are:

1.  **Central Banks:** The Fed, ECB, and BoJ. They manage inflation and interest rates. Their decisions move markets the most. When the Fed raises rates, the Dollar typically strengthens. When they cut rates, the Dollar weakens.
2.  **Commercial Banks:** The Liquidity Providers like J.P. Morgan, Deutsche Bank, and Citibank who handle the actual transactions. They facilitate the majority of daily Forex volume.
3.  **Hedge Funds:** Speculators who trade billions to profit from market shifts. George Soros famously made $1 billion in a single day by shorting the British Pound in 1992.
4.  **Retail Traders:** Individual traders like you, using platforms to catch a slice of the big moves. Retail traders account for about 5% to 7% of total Forex volume.

Real World Example: During the 2008 financial crisis, central banks around the world slashed interest rates to near zero. The US Dollar weakened significantly against currencies of countries with stronger economic fundamentals. Traders who understood this dynamic and positioned themselves accordingly profited enormously from the massive currency movements.

## The Mechanics: Pips, Lots, and Spreads

To speak the language of Forex, you need to understand three terms:

*   **Pip (Percentage in Point):** The smallest price move a currency can make. For EUR/USD, a move from 1.0850 to 1.0851 is 1 pip. For USD/JPY, a move from 110.50 to 110.51 is also 1 pip.
*   **Lot Size:** The volume of your trade. 1 Standard Lot is 100,000 units of the base currency. A Mini Lot is 10,000 units. A Micro Lot is 1,000 units.
*   **Spread:** The difference between the Buy price and the Sell price. This is the broker's fee for facilitating the trade. If EUR/USD has a Buy price of 1.1050 and a Sell price of 1.1048, the spread is 2 pips.

Real World Example: If you buy EUR/USD at a spread of 2 pips and the market moves 10 pips in your favor, your net profit is 8 pips (10 pips minus 2 pips spread). This is why traders prefer pairs with tight spreads like EUR/USD and USD/JPY, as the cost of trading is lower.

## The Secret to Forex: Liquidity

Liquidity refers to how easily you can enter and exit a trade without moving the price. The Major Pairs (EUR/USD, GBP/USD, USD/JPY, USD/CHF) have the highest liquidity. This means they have the lowest spreads and are the safest for new traders. Exotic Pairs like USD/TRY or EUR/ZAR have low liquidity, meaning they are expensive to trade and can have gaps in price that bypass your stop loss.

Real World Example: In January 2015, the Swiss National Bank suddenly removed the EUR/CHF peg. The Swiss Franc surged over 30% in minutes. Because of the extreme volatility and lack of liquidity, many brokers went bankrupt and traders using exotic pairs were unable to exit their positions, resulting in massive losses. This event highlighted why trading major pairs with high liquidity is safer for retail traders.

## The 24/5 Market Cycle

Forex is open 24 hours a day, 5 days a week. It follows the sun:

1.  **Sydney Session:** Quiet, low volatility. Best for beginners to practice without the noise of high volume.
2.  **Tokyo Session:** The Asian market kicks in. The Japanese Yen pairs are most active during this session.
3.  **London Session:** The heavy hitter. This is where the most volume occurs. Most major moves happen during the London session.
4.  **New York Session:** The overlap between London and New York is the most active time of the day. This is when the biggest opportunities and the highest volatility occur.

Real World Example: The London New York overlap (8:00 AM to 12:00 PM EST) typically accounts for over 50% of daily Forex volume. Traders who focus on this window can capitalize on the highest liquidity and the tightest spreads. If you are a scalper or day trader, this is the best time to trade.

## Why Most Forex Traders Fail

The failure rate in Forex is high because people treat it like a casino. They use 1:500 leverage without understanding that a 0.2% move against them can wipe out their account. Professional Forex trading is about probability and math. You win some, you lose some, and the goal is to ensure your wins are larger than your losses over a 100 trade sample size.

Real World Example: Studies show that 70% to 80% of retail Forex traders lose money. The main reasons are over leverage, lack of risk management, and emotional trading. A trader with a $1,000 account using 1:500 leverage to open a 1 lot position on EUR/USD is risking $10 per pip. A 100 pip move against them means a $1,000 loss, wiping out the entire account in minutes. Had they used proper position sizing and a Micro Lot, the same move would have cost only $10, and they could have survived to trade another day.

## Currency Pairs Classification: Majors, Minors, and Exotics

Understanding how currency pairs are classified is fundamental to your Forex education. Not all pairs are created equal, and each category carries distinct characteristics that affect your trading strategy, risk profile, and cost of doing business.

### Major Currency Pairs

Major pairs are the most traded currency pairs in the world and always include the US Dollar on one side. They represent the economies of the world's largest and most stable nations. The seven major pairs are EUR/USD, GBP/USD, USD/JPY, USD/CHF, AUD/USD, USD/CAD, and NZD/USD. These pairs account for approximately 80% of all daily Forex trading volume.

The defining characteristics of major pairs are tight spreads, deep liquidity, and relatively predictable price behavior. Because so many market participants are trading these pairs, the bid-ask spreads are minimized, which directly reduces your cost of entry and exit. For a retail trader, especially one just starting out, major pairs should be the cornerstone of your portfolio. EUR/USD alone accounts for roughly 25% of global Forex turnover, making it the single most liquid financial instrument on the planet.

Major pairs are also the most heavily researched by institutional analysts and economists. This means there is a wealth of high-quality data, commentary, and forecasts available to inform your trading decisions. As a senior market analyst, I always recommend that new traders focus exclusively on major pairs until they have demonstrated consistent profitability over at least three months of live or simulated trading.

### Minor Currency Pairs (Crosses)

Minor pairs, also known as crosses, are currency pairs that do not include the US Dollar. Examples include EUR/GBP, EUR/JPY, GBP/JPY, AUD/NZD, and EUR/AUD. Historically, these pairs had to be converted through the US Dollar, but modern Forex markets now quote them directly.

Minors tend to have wider spreads than majors and lower liquidity, though they are still sufficiently liquid for active trading. The key advantage of trading crosses is that they allow you to express a view on the relative strength of two non-US economies without the noise of Dollar-related headlines influencing the pair. For instance, if you believe the UK economy will outperform the Japanese economy based on domestic policy shifts, you can trade GBP/JPY directly.

However, minors can be more volatile than majors. GBP/JPY, for example, is known for its large daily ranges and sharp swings. This makes it attractive to experienced traders but dangerous for beginners. I always advise clients to approach crosses with smaller position sizes and wider stops until they understand the unique behavior of each cross pair.

### Exotic Currency Pairs

Exotic pairs consist of one major currency paired with the currency of an emerging or smaller economy. Examples include USD/TRY (US Dollar/Turkish Lira), USD/ZAR (US Dollar/South African Rand), EUR/SEK (Euro/Swedish Krona), and GBP/MXN (British Pound/Mexican Peso).

Exotics are characterized by significantly wider spreads, much lower liquidity, and extreme volatility. They are heavily influenced by local political events, commodity prices, and central bank interventions that may not be widely reported in mainstream financial media. For example, the Turkish Lira has experienced dramatic devaluations due to unconventional monetary policy decisions, creating both enormous risk and opportunity.

From a risk management perspective, exotics should be avoided by all but the most experienced traders. The wide spreads eat into potential profits, and the erratic price movements can trigger stop losses or even cause margin calls with little warning. I strongly recommend that traders build a solid foundation with majors and minors before even considering exotic pairs.

## How Forex Trading Works: Bid/Ask, Order Types, and Market Hours

### Understanding Bid and Ask Prices

Every currency pair quoted on your trading platform shows two prices: the Bid and the Ask. The Bid price is the price at which you can sell the base currency, and the Ask price is the price at which you can buy the base currency. The difference between these two prices is the spread, which I covered earlier as the broker's compensation.

When you look at a EUR/USD quote of 1.1050/1.1052, the first number (1.1050) is the Bid, meaning you can sell EUR/USD at that price. The second number (1.1052) is the Ask, meaning you can buy EUR/USD at that price. If you were to buy and immediately sell, you would incur a loss of 2 pips equal to the spread. This is why choosing brokers with competitive spreads is crucial to your long-term profitability.

### Order Types in Forex

Understanding order types is essential for executing your trading strategy effectively. The most common order types include:

*   **Market Orders:** These are executed immediately at the current market price. They guarantee execution but not price, which can lead to slippage during periods of high volatility or low liquidity.
*   **Limit Orders:** A Buy Limit order places a buy order below the current market price, anticipating that the price will dip before rising. A Sell Limit order places a sell order above the current market price, expecting the price to rise before falling. Limit orders guarantee price but not execution.
*   **Stop Orders:** A Buy Stop order places a buy order above the current market price, activated when the price rises to that level. A Sell Stop order places a sell order below the current market price, activated when the price falls to that level. These are commonly used for breakout strategies.
*   **Stop Loss Orders:** A protective order that automatically closes your position at a predetermined price to limit your loss. This is the single most important risk management tool available to you.
*   **Take Profit Orders:** An order that automatically closes your position when a specific profit target is reached, locking in your gains before the market can reverse.

### Forex Market Hours in Detail

The Forex market operates continuously across four major trading sessions, each aligned with the business hours of the world's primary financial centers. Understanding these sessions allows you to align your trading with periods of maximum opportunity.

The **Sydney Session** opens at 10:00 PM GMT and runs until 7:00 AM GMT. This is the quietest session, characterized by thin volume and limited directional movement. I generally advise traders to use this time for analysis, journaling, or planning rather than active trading.

The **Tokyo Session** (also called the Asian Session) runs from 12:00 AM GMT to 9:00 AM GMT. The Japanese Yen pairs, AUD/USD, and NZD/USD see increased activity during this window. The Tokyo session often sets the tone for the day, and breakouts from this session frequently carry through into the London session.

The **London Session** begins at 8:00 AM GMT and ends at 5:00 PM GMT. This is the most important session for Forex trading, accounting for approximately 35% to 40% of total daily volume. The majority of significant economic data releases from Europe occur during this window, making it a prime time for news-based trading strategies.

The **New York Session** runs from 1:00 PM GMT to 10:00 PM GMT. US economic data, including the all-important Non-Farm Payrolls report, is released during this session. The overlap between London and New York (1:00 PM to 5:00 PM GMT) is the absolute peak of Forex activity, with the highest volume, tightest spreads, and greatest volatility.

## Fundamental Analysis vs Technical Analysis

One of the most important decisions you will make as a Forex trader is choosing your analytical approach. The two primary schools of thought are Fundamental Analysis and Technical Analysis, and while they are often presented as opposing methodologies, the most successful traders integrate both.

### Fundamental Analysis

Fundamental analysis involves evaluating currencies based on economic data, monetary policy, geopolitical events, and overall macroeconomic conditions. The core premise is that a currency's value reflects the economic health and future prospects of its issuing country. If a country's economy is strong and expected to strengthen further, its currency should appreciate relative to currencies of weaker economies.

Fundamental analysts study central bank policies, inflation data, employment figures, trade balances, and political stability. They build a thesis about where a currency should be heading over weeks, months, or even years. For example, if the Federal Reserve signals a hawkish stance with plans for multiple rate hikes, a fundamental analyst would conclude that the US Dollar is likely to strengthen against currencies where central banks are dovish.

This approach requires a deep understanding of macroeconomics and the ability to interpret complex data releases in real time. It is particularly well-suited to position traders and swing traders who hold trades for days or weeks.

### Technical Analysis

Technical analysis focuses on studying price charts, patterns, and statistical indicators to forecast future price movements. The underlying philosophy is that all known information is already reflected in the price, and that historical price patterns tend to repeat themselves due to collective market psychology.

Technical analysts use tools such as support and resistance levels, moving averages, trend lines, Fibonacci retracements, and oscillators like the Relative Strength Index (RSI) and MACD. They identify chart patterns such as head and shoulders, double tops and bottoms, and flag formations to predict potential price continuations or reversals.

This approach is highly visual and works well for shorter timeframes, making it popular among scalpers and day traders. However, technical analysis is not infallible. A sudden news event can obliterate a perfectly formed technical setup in seconds, which is why risk management remains paramount regardless of your analytical method.

### The Professional Synthesis

As a senior market analyst, I advocate a blended approach. Use fundamental analysis to determine the directional bias — the "what" and "why" of your trade. Then use technical analysis to refine your entry and exit points — the "where" and "when." For instance, if fundamental data suggests the US Dollar should strengthen due to rising interest rates, look for technical confirmation on the charts, such as a breakout above a key resistance level on USD/JPY, before entering your position.

## Economic Indicators That Move Forex

Forex markets are driven by a continuous stream of economic data releases. Understanding which indicators have the most significant impact on currency prices is essential for any serious trader.

### Gross Domestic Product (GDP)

GDP is the broadest measure of a country's economic output and is considered the primary indicator of economic health. A rising GDP signals a growing economy, which tends to attract foreign investment and strengthen the domestic currency. Conversely, declining GDP can lead to currency weakness as investor confidence wanes.

GDP is typically reported quarterly and is closely watched by traders because it often sets the tone for central bank policy decisions. A surprisingly strong GDP print can trigger aggressive buying of a currency, while a disappointing figure can spark a selloff. Always compare actual GDP figures to market consensus estimates, as it is the deviation from expectations that moves markets, not the absolute number itself.

### Non-Farm Payrolls (NFP)

The US Non-Farm Payrolls report, released on the first Friday of every month, is arguably the single most impactful economic indicator in the Forex market. It measures the change in the number of employed people in the US, excluding the farming industry, government employees, and private household workers.

The NFP report provides critical insight into the health of the US labor market, which directly influences Federal Reserve policy decisions. A strong NFP figure suggests a robust economy and increases the likelihood of interest rate hikes, which strengthens the US Dollar. A weak NFP figure has the opposite effect. The NFP release routinely causes massive intraday volatility, with EUR/USD moving 100 to 200 pips within minutes of the announcement.

### Interest Rate Decisions

Interest rates are the most powerful driver of currency values in the long run. When a central bank raises interest rates, it makes holding that currency more attractive because investors earn higher returns on deposits and bonds denominated in that currency. This increased demand drives up the currency's value.

Central bank meetings and rate decisions are scheduled well in advance, and traders carefully parse every statement and speech from central bank officials for clues about future policy direction. The language used by central bankers, often referred to as "forward guidance," can move markets even more than the actual rate decision itself.

### Inflation Data (CPI and PPI)

Inflation measures, particularly the Consumer Price Index (CPI) and Producer Price Index (PPI), are critical because central banks use inflation data to guide monetary policy. Higher inflation typically leads to tighter monetary policy and higher interest rates, which strengthens the currency. Lower inflation can lead to accommodative policy and currency weakness.

### Other Key Indicators

Additional indicators that significantly impact Forex include the Purchasing Managers' Index (PMI), retail sales figures, trade balance data, consumer confidence indices, and manufacturing output. Each of these provides a piece of the economic puzzle that traders use to form their views on currency direction.

## Risk Management Basics for Forex

Risk management is not optional in Forex trading — it is the foundation upon which all successful trading careers are built. Without proper risk management, even the best trading strategy will eventually destroy your account.

### Position Sizing

Position sizing is the process of determining how many units or lots to trade based on your account size and the distance to your stop loss. The golden rule is to never risk more than 1% to 2% of your trading account on any single trade.

For example, if you have a $10,000 account, you should risk no more than $100 to $200 per trade. If your stop loss is 50 pips away, and you are trading EUR/USD where 1 pip on a micro lot equals $0.10, you would calculate your position size as follows: $100 risk divided by $5 per micro lot (50 pips × $0.10) equals 20 micro lots, or 0.2 standard lots.

### Risk-to-Reward Ratio

Every trade should have a clearly defined risk-to-reward ratio. This ratio compares the potential profit of a trade to the potential loss. A 1:2 risk-to-reward ratio means you are risking $1 to make $2. Professional traders aim for a minimum of 1:2, and many prefer 1:3 or better.

The beauty of a favorable risk-to-reward ratio is that you can be wrong more often than you are right and still be profitable. With a 1:3 risk-to-reward ratio, you only need to win 33% of your trades to break even. This is why disciplined traders focus as much on the potential reward as they do on the probability of winning.

### Stop Loss Discipline

Always use a stop loss on every trade, without exception. A stop loss is your insurance policy against catastrophic loss. It defines your maximum risk before you even enter the trade, removing the temptation to hold a losing position hoping it will recover.

Place your stop loss at a logical level based on market structure, not at an arbitrary distance. For a long trade, place the stop below a recent swing low or support level. For a short trade, place it above a recent swing high or resistance level. This ensures that your stop is hit only when the market invalidates your trade thesis, not on random noise.

### Leverage: The Double-Edged Sword

Leverage allows you to control a large position with a relatively small amount of capital. A leverage ratio of 1:100 means that for every $1 in your account, you can control $100 in the market. While this amplifies potential profits, it equally amplifies potential losses.

I have seen countless traders destroy their accounts with excessive leverage. The key is to use leverage as a tool, not a crutch. Professional traders typically use much less leverage than their brokers allow. A practical approach is to size your positions so that the effective leverage on your account never exceeds 5:1 or 10:1, regardless of what your broker offers.

### Diversification and Correlation

Avoid putting all your capital into a single trade or even a single currency pair. However, be aware of currency correlation. For example, EUR/USD and GBP/USD are positively correlated, meaning they tend to move in the same direction. Opening long positions on both pairs effectively doubles your exposure to the same risk. Understanding correlation helps you avoid unintended concentration of risk.

## Common Forex Trading Mistakes

Having analyzed thousands of trading accounts over my career, I have identified a consistent set of mistakes that plague retail Forex traders. Avoiding these pitfalls is often the difference between success and failure.

### Over-Leveraging

This is the number one mistake, particularly among beginners. Using maximum leverage offered by the broker gives a false sense of opportunity but creates a precarious account balance where even a small adverse move can trigger a margin call. Remember, leverage is not free money — it is borrowed capital that must be repaid.

### Trading Without a Plan

Many traders enter positions based on a gut feeling or a tip from a social media post without any analysis or predefined exit strategy. This is gambling, not trading. Every trade should have a clear entry, stop loss, take profit target, and rationale. If you cannot articulate why you are entering the trade, you should not be in the trade.

### Revenge Trading

After a losing trade, many traders immediately jump into the next position trying to recover their losses. This emotional reaction leads to impulsive decisions and usually results in additional losses. The market will always be there tomorrow. Step away, review what went wrong, and return with a clear mind.

### Ignoring the News Calendar

Trading during major economic releases without understanding the potential impact is reckless. Spreads widen dramatically, liquidity dries up, and price can gap through your stop loss. Either close your positions before high-impact news events or accept that the market will behave unpredictably.

### Overtrading

Not every day is a trading day. Some of the best decisions you will make are the ones where you choose not to trade. Overtrading leads to fatigue, poor decision-making, and excessive transaction costs from accumulated spreads. Quality over quantity is the mantra of professional trading.

### Neglecting to Review and Journal

Successful traders treat each trade as a learning experience. Without a trading journal, you cannot identify patterns in your behavior, track your progress, or refine your strategy. Record every trade, including the rationale, entry and exit points, emotional state, and outcome. Review it weekly.

## How to Choose a Broker

Selecting the right Forex broker is one of the most consequential decisions you will make. A bad broker can negate even the best trading strategy through poor execution, hidden fees, or questionable practices.

### Regulation and Security

The most important factor is regulation. Choose a broker regulated by a reputable authority such as the UK's Financial Conduct Authority (FCA), Australia's Australian Securities and Investments Commission (ASIC), the US National Futures Association (NFA), or Cyprus's CySEC. Regulation ensures that the broker maintains segregated client accounts, meets capital requirements, and provides a framework for dispute resolution.

### Spreads and Commissions

Compare the spreads offered by different brokers on your preferred currency pairs. Some brokers offer raw spreads with a commission per lot, while others offer wider spreads with no commission. Calculate the total cost of trading for your typical trade size and frequency to determine which pricing model is more economical for your strategy.

### Execution Quality

A broker's execution quality determines whether you get filled at the price you intended. Look for a broker that offers fast execution with minimal slippage and re-quotes. During high-impact news events, execution quality becomes even more critical. Some brokers offer Electronic Communication Network (ECN) or Straight Through Processing (STP) models, which route your orders directly to liquidity providers.

### Available Instruments and Platforms

Ensure the broker offers the currency pairs and other instruments you want to trade. Also, evaluate the trading platforms available. Most professional traders use MetaTrader 4 or MetaTrader 5, both of which are widely supported and offer extensive charting, automation, and analysis capabilities.

### Customer Support and Education

Responsive customer support is essential, especially if you encounter technical issues or have questions about your account. Many reputable brokers also offer educational resources, webinars, and market analysis. While these should not replace your own study, they can be valuable supplements to your learning.

### Deposit and Withdrawal Terms

Review the broker's deposit and withdrawal policies carefully. Check for minimum deposit requirements, available payment methods, processing times, and any associated fees. A broker that makes it difficult or expensive to withdraw your profits is a red flag.

## Building a Trading Plan

A trading plan is your blueprint for success in the Forex market. It removes emotion from decision-making and provides a framework for consistent, disciplined trading. Without a plan, you are navigating without a compass.

### Define Your Trading Goals

Start by establishing clear, measurable, and realistic goals. Ask yourself: What do I want to achieve in the next 6 months? What is my target monthly return? What level of drawdown am I willing to accept? Your goals should be specific enough that you can objectively evaluate your performance.

### Identify Your Trading Style

Determine whether you are a scalper, day trader, swing trader, or position trader. Each style requires different time commitments, analytical approaches, and risk tolerances. Scalpers may take dozens of trades per day with small profit targets, while position traders may hold trades for months based on macroeconomic trends. Choose a style that aligns with your lifestyle, personality, and availability.

### Select Your Markets and Timeframes

Based on your trading style and risk tolerance, select the currency pairs and timeframes you will focus on. As I mentioned earlier, major pairs are the safest starting point. For timeframes, shorter timeframes (1-minute to 1-hour) suit scalpers and day traders, while higher timeframes (4-hour, daily, weekly) suit swing and position traders.

### Establish Entry and Exit Rules

Your trading plan should specify the exact conditions that must be met before entering a trade. This might include a specific technical pattern, a confluence of indicators, or a fundamental catalyst. Similarly, define your exit rules, including where you will place your stop loss and take profit for each trade.

### Set Risk Parameters

Define your maximum risk per trade, maximum daily loss, and maximum weekly drawdown. These rules protect you from catastrophic losses during periods of poor performance or emotional instability. Once you hit your daily loss limit, stop trading for the day. This discipline will save your account.

### Document Your Routine

Your trading plan should include a daily routine that covers pre-market analysis, scheduled trades, journaling, and post-market review. A consistent routine builds discipline and ensures that you are approaching the market with a prepared and focused mindset every day.

### Review and Adapt

A trading plan is a living document. Review it regularly, ideally on a monthly basis, and make adjustments based on your performance data and evolving market conditions. If a particular strategy is consistently underperforming, do not stubbornly stick with it. Adapt and evolve as the market demands.

## FAQ: Forex Basics

**Q: How much money do I need to start Forex trading?**

A: You can start with as little as $100 using Micro Lots (0.01), but $1,000 to $5,000 is recommended for proper risk management. The more capital you have, the more flexibility you have in sizing your positions correctly.

**Q: Is Forex trading legal?**

A: Yes, in most countries. However, you must use a regulated broker to ensure your funds are protected. Always verify that your broker is regulated by a reputable authority such as the FCA, ASIC, or NFA.

**Q: Can I trade Forex on my phone?**

A: Yes, but professional analysis should always be done on a larger screen where you can see the full market structure. Use mobile trading for execution, but perform your analysis on a desktop where you can see the bigger picture.
